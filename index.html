Come, let’s go and look at something.

Why

I love children, and I take their growing up seriously.

I have always been at ease with children. Their honesty, their questions, their way of being entirely in the moment. Caring for them is the work I am most myself in.

I believe that character is formed early and quietly, in the company of adults who are patient, truthful and kind. A day in my care is a day of that company. We look at beautiful things properly. We treat the museum guard and the ducks and each other with the same courtesy. We say thank you. None of it is taught as a lesson; it is simply how the day is lived.

I keep Wonder Nanny small so that it stays personal. For now, I take every day myself.

For clarity: I am a private nanny, not a nursery or registered childcare, so childcare allowance does not apply. I hold a certificate of conduct (VOG) and paediatric first aid, I am insured for the care of children, and my references are available on request.

How a day is shaped

Six fixed points.

  1. Arrival. I kneel down and introduce myself. I show your child what is in my bag: a sketchbook, pencils, and one question for the day. What is the oldest thing we will see today?
  2. Out. At your child’s pace. My phone stays in my bag.
  3. A meal. Something real, organic where possible, according to your instructions.
  4. Rest. A bench, a story, a drawing of what we saw.
  5. One beautiful thing. Before we return I ask what was most beautiful today, and write it down in your child’s words.
  6. Handover. Your child, the card, and a few photographs of the day. Nothing is ever shared beyond you.

Who comes

For now, me. In time, a small circle.

Every day is currently taken by me personally. Should Wonder Nanny grow, it will grow slowly: with people who have first spent a day working beside me, who hold a certificate of conduct and paediatric first aid, whose references I have taken myself, and who make the same undertaking I do.

I am fully present. I am patient. I take a child only where I would take my own. I tell the parents the truth about the day. What I see in a home remains there.

Discretion is part of the work. I do not speak about the families I work for, and I will not speak about yours. If you are a nanny who recognises yourself in these lines, I would be glad to hear from you. Write to me.

The tenth

Ten percent of every day goes to children who have no one.

I help build a home for orphaned children in Bugiri, Uganda: Family of Pure Grace. Ten percent of every day you book goes there, and appears as a line on your invoice. Every quarter I publish what has been transferred. For every ten days booked, I also give one afternoon to a mother in my neighbourhood who is raising her children alone. This is not a marketing gesture. It is why I work.

Questions

Answered plainly.

Is this registered childcare?

No. I am a private nanny, not a gastouderbureau or kinderopvang in the national register, so childcare allowance does not apply. I hold a certificate of conduct (VOG) and paediatric first aid, and I am insured for the care of children.

What happens to the photographs?

They are yours. I never publish a child’s face, and nothing at all without your written consent. With your permission I may show a drawing or a card, without names.

What if it rains?

Every outdoor day has an indoor alternative. We decide together the evening before.

What are the cancellation terms?

Free of charge until 48 hours before. Within 48 hours, half the price, as the day was held for you. If your child is unwell, no charge.

Do you care for babies?

By arrangement, one at a time, after we have met. The days above are written for ages three to twelve.

Who are you?

Wietske Hoencamp. I live in Amsterdam. I am a health and lifestyle coach, a photographer and a ski instructor, and I am reading psychology. I have cared for the children of more than thirty families. Wonder Nanny is part of L’arte dell’amore, where the rest of my work lives.
