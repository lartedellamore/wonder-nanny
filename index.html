A day of wonder, while you are away.

Why I do this

Children ask the best questions. I wanted to spend my days answering them.

For years I have looked after other people’s children, in Amsterdam and far from it. I noticed that the hours parents are away are often hours a child simply waits out. I think they can be more than that. Not louder or busier — just more alive. A windmill from the inside. The oldest painting in the room. Dough under small hands.

Wonder Nanny is one person, for now: me. If it grows, it grows slowly, with people I trust and have worked beside. My faith is Catholic and it shapes how I see every child — as a wonder, not a task. Every family is welcome here. You do not have to share my faith for me to love your child well for a day.

I am not a nursery or a registered childcare provider, and I will not pretend to be. I hold a certificate of conduct and paediatric first aid, I am insured for the care of children, and I will always tell you the truth about how the day went.

How a day goes

Six small things, every time.

  1. We begin. I bring a sketchbook and pencils, and one question for the day. Today’s might be: what is the oldest thing we will see?
  2. We go. At your child’s pace. My phone stays in my bag, except for a few photographs.
  3. We eat. Something real, organic where I can manage it.
  4. We rest. Twenty quiet minutes: drawing, a story, a bench.
  5. One beautiful thing. I ask your child to name it. I write it down in their words.
  6. I hand over. A card with the day on it, and the photographs, in your inbox that evening.

Who looks after your child

Me. And, in time, a few people I trust.

For now I take every booking myself. If one day I cannot, and someone else comes, she will have walked a day beside me first, hold a certificate of conduct and first aid for children, and have sat at my table. She will have made the same promise I make:

I am here, fully. I am patient. I take a child only where I would take my own. I tell the parents the truth about the day. What I see in a home stays there.

If this sounds like you, I would like to meet you. Wonder Nanny grows out of Catholic faith and is open to anyone who lives this way. Write to me.

The gift

A tenth of every day goes to children who have no one.

Ten percent of every booking goes to the Family of Pure Grace home for orphaned children in Bugiri, Uganda. You will see it as a line on your invoice. Every few months I will show what has been sent. And for every ten days I am booked, I give one afternoon away to a mother in my parish who is raising her children alone. I do this because it is the reason I work at all.

Things you may want to know

Plainly.

Is this registered childcare?

No. I am a private nanny, not a gastouderbureau or kinderopvang in the national register, so you cannot claim childcare allowance. I hold a certificate of conduct (VOG) and paediatric first aid, and I am insured for looking after children.

What happens to the photographs?

They are yours. I never publish a child’s face, and nothing at all without your written yes. With your permission I might show a sketchbook page or a card, without names.

And if it rains?

Every outdoor day has an indoor twin. We decide together the evening before.

Can I cancel?

Yes, free of charge until 48 hours before. Within 48 hours I ask half the price, because the day was kept for you. If your child is ill, nothing, ever.

Do you look after babies?

Sometimes, one at a time, after we have met. The days above are written for ages three to twelve.

Who are you?

Wietske Hoencamp. I live in Amsterdam, coach, photograph, teach skiing and study psychology. Wonder Nanny grew out of L’arte dell’amore, the small house where the rest of my work lives.
