Why I do this

Because children are the best company I know.

I love children. How honest they are, how they ask the question everyone else is thinking, how a puddle can be the whole afternoon. With a child I am completely where I am.

I also care about who they become. Children learn kindness by being treated kindly, patience from someone who waits with them, courage by being allowed to climb a little higher with a hand nearby. A day with me is a day of being loved well, looking at beautiful things, saying thank you to the lady at the museum, and sharing the last pancake. That is where good people begin.

Wonder Nanny is small on purpose: it is me, with a sketchbook in my bag and a plan for the day.

I am a private nanny, not registered childcare, so childcare allowance doesn’t apply. I have a certificate of conduct, first aid for children and insurance, and you are welcome to speak with families I have worked for.

How a day with me goes

Six things that happen every time.

  1. We say hello. I ask your child’s name and open my bag: a sketchbook, pencils, and a question for the day. What is the oldest thing we will see today?
  2. We go. At your child’s pace. My phone stays in my bag.
  3. We eat. Something real, organic when I can, and only what you have said is fine.
  4. We rest. A bench, a story, a drawing of what we saw.
  5. One beautiful thing. Before we head home I ask what was most beautiful today, and write it down just as they say it.
  6. I bring your child home. With the card and a few photographs of the day. Your child’s face never goes online.

Who comes to the door

Me. And one day, perhaps, a few people I love working with.

For now, when you book a day, I am the one who rings the bell. If Wonder Nanny grows, it will be with people who have spent a day beside me first, have a certificate of conduct and first aid for children, and make the same promise:

I am here, all of me. I am patient. I take a child only where I would take my own. I tell the parents the truth about the day, the lovely parts and the scraped knee. What I see in a home stays there.

The gift

A tenth of every day goes to children who have no one to tuck them in.

I help build a home for orphaned children in Bugiri, Uganda: Family of Pure Grace. Ten percent of every day you book goes there; you will see it on your invoice. Every few months I show what has been sent. And for every ten days I am booked, I give one afternoon to a mother raising her children alone. I don’t do this work for the money. I do it because love should go somewhere.

Things parents ask me

Honest answers.

Is this registered childcare?

No. I am a private nanny, not a gastouderbureau or kinderopvang in the national register, so you can’t claim childcare allowance. I do have a certificate of conduct (VOG), first aid for children, and insurance for looking after them.

What happens to the photographs?

They are yours. I never put a child’s face online, and nothing at all without your written yes. With your permission I might show a drawing or a card, without names.

And if it rains?

Every outdoor day has an indoor twin; we choose together the evening before.

Can I cancel?

Of course, free until 48 hours before. Within 48 hours I ask half, because I kept the day for you. If your child is ill, nothing at all.

Do you look after babies?

Sometimes, one at a time, once we have met. The days above are written for ages three to twelve.

Who are you?

Wietske Hoencamp, Amsterdam. I have looked after the children of more than thirty families. I also coach, photograph and teach skiing; the rest of my work lives at L’arte dell’amore.
